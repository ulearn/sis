● The framing for the conversation                                                                                                                                                         
                                      
  Three things make this a "no blunt force trauma" conversation:                                                                                                                           
   
  1. Make the policy the bad guy, not him.                                                                                                                                                 
  Don't open with "you didn't do enough cover." Open with "I want to formalise something that's been implicit: cover defaults to the DOS where capacity exists, and we use hourly staff as
  overflow." That reframes it from a personal critique to a policy you're rolling out — for everyone, going forward. He didn't do anything wrong; the system just wasn't explicit. He      
  becomes the implementer of the new norm rather than the target of it.
                                                                                                                                                                                           
  2. Make the data the messenger.                                                                                                                                                          
  Once the policy exists, just show him the numbers each month. "April: 67h of cover. You did 14h (21%). Hourly cover paid: ~€1,165." No commentary needed. The number IS the conversation.
   If next month he picks up more, the dashboard reflects it. If he doesn't, the dashboard reflects that too — and now there's a standing question to discuss without you having to raise  
  it as a complaint each time.                           
                                                                                                                                                                                           
  3. Give him a fair out.                                                                                                                                                                  
  The risk in this conversation is he reads it as "we don't think you're doing anything." He'll defend, and you'll both end up entrenched. Pre-empt it: "If your week was full of LMS work,
   observations, agent meetings, parent calls, mentoring — I want to know that, because right now I can't see it." That's not rhetorical — it's a genuine ask. It also quietly invites him 
  to articulate his output, which is the question you actually want answered. If the list comes back thin, that's its own data point. If it comes back full, great — you've learnt
  something and validated the salary.                                                                                                                                                      
                                                         
  The infrastructure to support it

  I'd add three small things, in order of effort:                                                                                                                                          
   
  (a) Cover Dashboard tile on the Monthly Payroll tab — small, ships in an hour:                                                                                                           
  Cover this period: 67.0h                               
    ├ DOS:           14.0h  (21%)  ← target ≥50% where capacity exists                                                                                                                     
    ├ Hourly staff:  53.0h  (79%)  ≈ €1,165                                                                                                                                                
    └ Sick/other:     0.0h                                                                                                                                                                 
  That single tile, refreshed every payroll cycle, is the conversation going forward. You don't have to bring it up — it's just there in the report.                                       
                                                                                                                                                                                           
  (b) A "DOS capacity floor" target — a single number in admin settings, e.g. "Expect DOS to absorb ≥50% of cover hours per period." The dashboard then shows green/amber/red against that 
  target. You set it once, with him, in the kick-off conversation. Now it's his target, not your judgment.                                                                                 
                                                                                                                                                                                           
  (c) A weekly DOS log — this is the touchier one, save for Phase 2. A simple form: "This week I spent X hours on: [LMS] [Observations] [Agent/Parent] [Cover] [Admin] [Other]." Five      
  entries a week, two minutes each. It gives him a place to demonstrate value; it gives you a place to verify it. Frame as "I want to know what to defend when stakeholders ask what the
  DOS does" — that's a generous frame that's also true.                                                                                                                                    
                                                         
  Suggested opening line for the conversation

  ▎  I'm not raising this to complain about April — I'm raising it because I want us to set a target going forward and look at it together each month. If your week's full of other stuff 
  ▎ I'm not seeing, tell me what — I'd like to make that visible too."                                                                                                                     
                                                         
  That's calm, data-anchored, forward-looking, and gives him room to respond without being cornered.
