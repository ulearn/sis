Modes of Transport in Dublin

There are 3 principal modes of public transport in Dublin:

• Dublin Bus
(https://www.dublinbus.ie/Your-Journey1/Mobileapps/)
• LUAS (tram)
(https://www.luas.ie/)
• DART & Irish Rail
(http://www.irishrail.ie/timetables/apps)

Dublin Bus is the most common way students come to and from school. Bus routes are good and busses have exclusive use of bus lanes during rush hour. Most bus stops also feature Real Time Information so people know how long they have to wait. 

The LUAS tram has two lines (Red running east-west and Green running north-south) but construction on the Green line to connect it to the Red line and extend it further north-westwill be completed by the end of 2018.

The DART rail line runs north-south along the Irish coast. It runs right through the city centre and links up with other inter-city rail services calling at Connolly Station and Tara/Pearse Street. All other intercity rail services coming into Dublin stop at Heuston Station. Passengers can then use the LUAS Red line, Dublin Bus, or Dublin Bikes to travel into Dublin city. 

Transport for Ireland has one or two apps which may be helpful for students planning their journeys.
https://www.transportforireland.ie/available-apps/ 

Leap Cards

It can be difficult to pay for all these transports in cash all the time as every transport method has different fares/ In order to integrate things and make it easier for customers, a unified transport card like the Oyster Card in London or the Suica card in Tokyo was introduced. 

A Leap Card (https://about.leapcard.ie/about) is a plastic smart card that is used to pay for public transport in Dublin. Students "top-up" the card with money at one of the stations or registered shops. 

Leap Card customers pay less than customers using cash on Dublin Bus, Luas, DART and commuter rail services. Students generally save about 20%+. Students can also save money on some other bus services.

Where can I buy a Leap Card and how much does it cost?

Students can buy a Leap Card at the Dublin Bus Head Office, 59 O’Connell Street (near the Spire) or in newsagent shops such as SPAR, CENTRA, and LONDIS. 

The card costs €5.00 but students can get this money back before they leave Ireland by returning  the card to the Dublin Bus Office (59 O'Connell St.). 

How can students add money to their Leap Card?

Students add money to their card in the same way they would add credit to their mobile phone.

There are three ways to do this. 
1. Show the Leap Card at one of the shops listed above and simply ask them to top it up by €5 or €10 or €20 or €50 or more. Pay them the money and wait while they top up your card.
2. Go to a LUAS tram stop and use one of the machines there to top the card up. Simply hold the card to the machine and follow the sintructions on the screen.
3. Use the Leap app (https://about.leapcard.ie/leap-top-up-app)

Types of Leap Card

Adult Leap Card

A €5 refundable deposit is charged for Adult Leap Cards. When buying a Leap Card students must top it up with €5 credit minimum. No identification is required for this type of card and hodlers will be charged (slightly cheaper) adult prices on whatever mode of transport they use.

Student Travel Card

If a students wishes to buy a Student Leap card, they will be asked to also buy a Student Travel Card which functions as an identification card. Students who buy this type of Leap Card will be charged student fees on whatever mode of transport they use. If a person has a Student Leap card but cannot produce the Student identification when asked, they may have to pay a fine / penalty

Where can people buy a Student Identity & Travel Card?

Students Identity and Travel Cards can be bought in the Dublin University Student Travel office in Trinity College (Door No. 6 of the Student Union).

The office opens Monday to Friday from 9:00 am to 5 pm.
 
The cost of the card is €15. A member of the staff there will provide students with a form that they will have to complete. They will also take a picture and will produce the card there and then.

Cycling

A small, relatively flat city with a temperate climate, Dublin is a perfect place to cycle and many students use a bike for transport once they become accustomed to the geography of the city. 

In the city centre, the Dublin Bikes scheme mentioned above is a free bicycle scheme. Students can use enter their information into the Dublin Bikes system (which requires a €150 guarantee on your credit card). Bikes can then be used for free up to 30 minutes. If you go over 30 minutes, you pay only a very small amount. This makes this mode of trasnport perfect for getting around the city. 

Students who do decide to cycle should be mindful of the dangers that exist on Irish roads. Students should begin cycling on routes and roads they know and when they feel comfortable sharing the road with cars and busses. Students should remember to remain visible and safe at all times. Use lights and reflective clothing - especially at night. 

Finally, if you do decide to cycle and you buy a bike, please remember to buy a lock as well. Bike theft is a problem in all parts of Dublin but especially in the city centre.
